Assurance · Operating effectiveness

Enterprise AI Assurance

A practical operating model for proving that AI governance, risk, data, security, model, vendor, and runtime controls are designed appropriately, operating effectively, and supported by decision-ready evidence.

Executive decision summary

Assurance provides independent, evidence-based confidence that important controls are suitably designed and continue to work in operation.

Business outcome

Give accountable leaders a defensible view of control effectiveness, unresolved findings, and residual risk.

How it works

Define control objectives, owners, tests, evidence, results, remediation, retesting, and reporting.

Decision rule

Do not treat a policy, design, certification, or self-attestation as proof that a control works in the relevant use case.

Related terms: evaluation, RACI, and production readiness.

Assurance operating model

From control intent to defensible assurance

Assurance is broader than a technical review. It connects lifecycle decisions, control ownership, evidence quality, independent challenge, findings, remediation, and executive reporting into a repeatable enterprise capability.

Core position
A control is not assured because it appears in a policy or architecture diagram. Assurance requires a defined objective, accountable owner, testable procedure, retained evidence, evaluated result, and traceable remediation where the control does not perform as intended.

Assurance mandate and coverage

The assurance plan should follow enterprise risk and intended use—not model novelty alone. Coverage spans the complete AI system, including business process, data, model, prompts, retrieval, applications, infrastructure, vendors, people, approvals, monitoring, and change.

01 Scope

Define the assurance universe

Systems, use cases, models, agents, vendors, jurisdictions, owners, and critical dependencies.

02 Prioritize

Set risk-based coverage

Decision impact, data sensitivity, action authority, scale, exposure, and control maturity.

03 Test

Evaluate controls

Design adequacy, implementation evidence, operating performance, and outcome testing.

04 Conclude

Form an assurance opinion

Ready, conditional, restricted, or stop—supported by evidence and residual-risk rationale.

05 Improve

Track remediation

Findings, owners, target dates, retesting, closure evidence, and framework improvements.

Three Lines responsibilities

Clear roles prevent self-certification from being mistaken for independent assurance and stop multiple teams from repeatedly asking for the same evidence.

First line

Own and operate

Business, product, engineering, data, model, security, and operations teams implement controls, perform routine checks, retain evidence, identify issues, and remediate weaknesses.

Second line

Set standards and challenge

Risk, compliance, privacy, legal, security governance, model risk, and AI governance establish policy, review risk decisions, challenge evidence, monitor portfolios, and test selected controls.

Third line

Provide independent assurance

Internal audit independently evaluates governance, control design, operating effectiveness, risk management, and the reliability of management and second-line conclusions.

Independence principle
The same team may provide advice during design and still perform a later review, but the assurance conclusion must disclose its role, preserve objective challenge, and distinguish management testing from independent evaluation.

Evidence and control testing

Evidence must prove that a specific control operated for the relevant system, environment, version, period, and decision—not simply that a generic process exists.

Control design

Control objective, mapped obligation or risk, owner, frequency, trigger, procedure, systems involved, evidence produced, and failure response.

Implementation evidence

Approved configuration, policy rules, access model, workflow, code, deployment record, vendor commitment, and assigned accountability.

Operating evidence

Logs, approvals, tickets, monitoring results, exception records, samples, attestations, incident actions, and retained decision artifacts.

Outcome evidence

Performance, bias, robustness, safety, override, complaint, loss, customer-impact, and value-realization measures against thresholds.

Findings, reporting, and continuous improvement

Findings

Describe the control failure

State the condition, expected criterion, cause, risk and impact, affected systems, evidence, severity, and required corrective action.

Remediation

Make accountability executable

Assign an accountable owner, target date, interim safeguards, dependencies, milestones, escalation route, and closure evidence.

Reporting

Support decisions at each level

Provide system conclusions, portfolio themes, overdue actions, risk concentrations, recurring failures, exceptions, and management responses.

Improvement

Feed lessons back into governance

Update policies, patterns, tests, thresholds, training, architecture guardrails, vendor requirements, and lifecycle gates.

Closure standard
A finding closes only after corrective action is implemented, evidence is retained, the relevant control is retested, residual risk is evaluated, and an authorized party accepts the closure conclusion.

Where technical review fits

The Security & Architecture Review is one focused assurance activity. Enterprise AI Assurance provides the broader operating model that determines scope, independence, evidence, conclusions, remediation, and ongoing reporting across the full AI lifecycle.
