Reference

AI & Technology Glossary

Clear definitions for the technical, governance, security, architecture, and delivery terms used across this portal.

ACDEGHILMPRSTV

A

Agent

AI and model concepts

Meaning
Software that uses a model to decide which permitted action to take toward a goal.
Why it matters
An agent can affect systems and data, so its authority and limits must be explicit.
Typical use
Research, service support, workflow coordination, and controlled tool use.
Related
Large Language Model, Model Context Protocol, Human-in-the-Loop.

Agentic AI

Artificial Intelligence with action authority

Meaning
AI that can plan steps, use tools, and take actions with some independence.
Why it matters
More autonomy can increase value and risk at the same time.
Typical use
Multi-step work where permissions, monitoring, and escalation are defined.
Related
Agentic AI Governance, Guardrail.

Application Programming Interface (API)

Architecture and integration

Meaning
A defined way for software systems to exchange requests and responses.
Why it matters
APIs form control boundaries for identity, data, rate limits, and audit records.
Typical use
Connecting applications, model services, data sources, and tools.
Related
IAM, SLA.

Attribute-Based Access Control (ABAC)

Security and privacy

Meaning
Access decisions based on attributes such as user, data type, location, and risk.
Why it matters
It supports more precise controls than a role alone.
Typical use
Restricting sensitive data or actions by context.
Related
RBAC, Least privilege.

C

Control plane

Governance and architecture

Meaning
The shared services that apply policy, identity, routing, monitoring, and approval rules.
Why it matters
It creates consistent controls across models and applications.
Typical use
Model gateways, policy enforcement, logging, and access management.
Related
Runtime implementation, Guardrail.

D

Data Loss Prevention (DLP)

Security and privacy

Meaning
Controls that detect and prevent sensitive information from leaving approved boundaries.
Why it matters
AI prompts and outputs can expose regulated or confidential data.
Typical use
Inspecting uploads, prompts, responses, email, and downloads.
Related
Data Security, PII.

E

Embedding

Retrieval and data

Meaning
A numerical representation that lets a system compare content by meaning.
Why it matters
It enables semantic search but may encode sensitive information or bias.
Typical use
Finding relevant passages for Retrieval-Augmented Generation.
Related
RAG, Vector database.

Evaluation

Assurance and operations

Meaning
A repeatable test of whether an AI system meets defined quality, safety, and business criteria.
Why it matters
Demonstrations do not prove reliable production performance.
Typical use
Testing before release and monitoring after change.
Related
AI Assurance, Production readiness.

G

Guardrail

Governance and assurance

Meaning
A technical or procedural limit that prevents, detects, or contains unacceptable behaviour.
Why it matters
Guardrails translate policy into enforceable boundaries.
Typical use
Content filters, action limits, approval gates, and output validation.
Related
HITL, Least privilege.

H

Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)

Governance and assurance

Meaning
A named person reviews or approves an AI decision or action at a defined point.
Why it matters
Human review is useful only when the person has time, authority, and sufficient evidence.
Typical use
High-impact decisions, exceptions, payments, and external communications.
Related
Guardrail, RACI.

I

Identity and Access Management (IAM)

Security and privacy

Meaning
The processes and technology used to identify users and control their access.
Why it matters
Every AI action should be attributable to an approved identity.
Typical use
Sign-in, permissions, service identities, and access reviews.
Related
RBAC, Least privilege.

Inference

AI and model concepts

Meaning
The process of running a trained model to produce a prediction or response.
Why it matters
Inference choices affect cost, speed, privacy, and reliability.
Typical use
Generating text, classifying documents, or making predictions.
Related
LLM, Model runtime.

L

Large Language Model (LLM)

AI and model concepts

Meaning
A model trained on large text collections to generate and analyse language.
Why it matters
Its responses are probabilistic and require evidence, testing, and controls.
Typical use
Drafting, extraction, summarisation, question answering, and agent reasoning.
Related
RAG, Evaluation.

Least privilege

Security and privacy

Meaning
Giving a person or system only the access needed for the approved task.
Why it matters
It limits damage from mistakes, misuse, or compromised credentials.
Typical use
Tool permissions, data access, service accounts, and agent actions.
Related
RBAC, ABAC.

M

Model Context Protocol (MCP)

Agents, skills, and tools

Meaning
A protocol that standardises how AI applications connect to tools and context sources.
Why it matters
A common connection method still requires identity, permission, validation, and audit controls.
Typical use
Connecting assistants and agents to enterprise services.
Related
Agent, API.

Model drift

Operations and observability

Meaning
A decline or change in model performance as data, behaviour, or conditions change.
Why it matters
A system that passed its original tests may later produce unacceptable results.
Typical use
Monitoring quality metrics and triggering review or rollback.
Related
Evaluation, Production readiness.

Model runtime

Architecture and infrastructure

Meaning
The environment and services used to execute a model in operation.
Why it matters
Runtime design determines security boundaries, performance, resilience, and cost.
Typical use
Hosted model endpoints, private infrastructure, or managed AI platforms.
Related
Runtime Implementation, Inference.

P

Personally Identifiable Information (PII)

Security and privacy

Meaning
Information that identifies, relates to, or can reasonably be linked to a person.
Why it matters
Its collection and use may create privacy, security, and regulatory obligations.
Typical use
Classifying data before it is sent to a model or external service.
Related
DLP, Data Security.

Prompt injection

Security and privacy

Meaning
Instructions designed to make an AI system ignore its intended rules or reveal information.
Why it matters
Untrusted content can influence tool use, data access, and output.
Typical use
Threat modelling and testing systems that process external content.
Related
Guardrail, Security Review.

Production readiness

Delivery and programme management

Meaning
Evidence that a system is safe, supportable, monitored, recoverable, and owned before live use.
Why it matters
A successful pilot does not prove that an organisation can operate the system reliably.
Typical use
Release gates covering risk, support, security, performance, and rollback.
Related
Evaluation, SLA.

R

RACI

Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed

Meaning
A responsibility model that identifies who performs work, owns the decision, provides input, and receives updates.
Why it matters
AI controls fail when ownership and decision rights are unclear.
Typical use
Lifecycle gates, incidents, exceptions, and model approvals.
Related
Governance Integration, HITL.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

Retrieval and data

Meaning
A method that finds relevant source material and gives it to a model when producing a response.
Why it matters
It can improve grounding, but access controls and source quality still determine what the model sees.
Typical use
Question answering over approved enterprise documents.
Related
Embedding, Vector database.

Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

Security and privacy

Meaning
Access granted according to a person's or system's approved role.
Why it matters
It makes permissions easier to govern and review.
Typical use
Separating administrator, reviewer, operator, and user permissions.
Related
ABAC, IAM.

Robotic Process Automation (RPA)

Automation

Meaning
Software that follows defined rules to perform repetitive user-interface or system tasks.
Why it matters
RPA is predictable for stable rules but is not the same as reasoning or autonomous action.
Typical use
Data entry, reconciliation, and structured workflow steps.
Related
Automation Spectrum, Agentic AI.

S

Security Information and Event Management (SIEM)

Security and operations

Meaning
A system that collects and analyses security events from multiple sources.
Why it matters
AI services need observable activity and actionable alerts.
Typical use
Central logging, detection, investigation, and incident response.
Related
Operational Risk, SLA.

Service-Level Agreement (SLA)

Operations and vendor management

Meaning
A documented commitment for service performance, availability, or response.
Why it matters
It makes operational expectations measurable and enforceable.
Typical use
Availability, support response, recovery, and vendor performance.
Related
Vendor Assurance, Production readiness.

Skill

Agents, skills, and tools

Meaning
A defined capability, instruction set, or workflow an AI agent can use.
Why it matters
Skills should have clear inputs, permissions, expected outputs, and owners.
Typical use
Standardising repeatable agent work.
Related
Agent, MCP.

T

Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)

Delivery and programme management

Meaning
The full cost to acquire, implement, operate, support, change, and retire a system.
Why it matters
Model fees alone do not represent the cost of a production AI service.
Typical use
Platform, vendor, and architecture decisions.
Related
Adoption Strategy, SLA.

V

Vector database

Retrieval and data

Meaning
A database designed to store and search numerical representations of content.
Why it matters
Its access rules, retention, and refresh process affect what an AI system can retrieve.
Typical use
Semantic search and Retrieval-Augmented Generation.
Related
Embedding, RAG.
