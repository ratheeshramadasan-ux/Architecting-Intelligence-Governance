Enterprise AI Gateway

A governed control point between enterprise applications and AI services that applies identity, policy, routing, safety, cost and evidence controls to every request and response.

1. Executive Summary

An AI Gateway solves the enterprise problem of uncontrolled, inconsistent and untraceable access to models. Applications use one governed interface while the gateway selects approved providers, enforces policy, protects data, records evidence and manages service quality.

What it is

A logical control plane deployed as a resilient service, not a single appliance or network proxy.

Why it matters

It turns AI policy into enforceable runtime behaviour across applications, models and providers.

Decision rule

Use it when several teams, models or risk levels require consistent control. Avoid it when it would add a redundant hop without meaningful policy or operational value.

Related: AI control plane, AI infrastructure, data security, Agentic AI, and control plane definition.

2. Business Value

Faster adoptionGive product teams one approved route to models instead of repeating provider, security and legal integration.
Risk reductionApply data classification, content controls, provider restrictions and audit rules consistently.
Cost controlMeasure consumption by application, business unit, model, provider and outcome before invoices arrive.
Operational resilienceUse tested fallback, rate limiting and circuit breaking without modifying every consuming application.
Measurable outcomes
Track time to onboard a new AI use case, percentage of traffic through approved routes, policy violation rate, cost per successful outcome, provider failover success, response quality and audit evidence completeness.

Architecture boundary
The gateway governs access to AI services. It does not replace enterprise identity, API management, data-loss prevention, model evaluation, vector databases or workflow engines; it integrates their decisions into the AI request path.

5. Implementation Approach

▶ Walk Through This Process
  1. Discover: identify every application, model endpoint, provider credential, data class and direct connection that must be governed.
  2. Define: agree service ownership, risk tiers, permitted models, routing rules, evidence requirements and measurable service objectives.
  3. Build: connect identity, secrets, policy, providers, observability and evaluation before onboarding production traffic.
  4. Pilot: route representative low-risk requests through the gateway and compare quality, latency, cost and control evidence with the current path.
  5. Migrate: move consumers in controlled waves, revoke direct provider credentials and keep a tested rollback route.
  6. Operate: monitor service health, policy effectiveness, quality, cost, incidents and business outcomes; improve controls using evidence.

Prerequisites

Decisions before technology

Approved use-case classes, enterprise identity, model catalogue, data classification, accountable service owner, logging destination and support model.

Dependencies

Services the gateway relies on

Identity provider, secrets manager, policy engine, provider endpoints, network controls, observability platform, evaluation service and configuration pipeline.

Migration

Move without breaking consumers

Offer a compatible API, dual-run selected traffic, compare quality and latency, preserve correlation IDs, test rollback and retire direct credentials.

Rollout

Expand by risk and capability

Start with observable low-risk traffic, then enable sensitive data, tools, agents and high-impact use cases only after controls are proven.

7. Security

AuthenticationValidate user and workload identities; reject anonymous production access.
AuthorisationEvaluate use case, model, tool, data class, tenant, region and action scope.
EncryptionUse TLS in transit, managed encryption at rest and controlled key ownership.
SecretsUse a secrets manager, short-lived provider credentials and automated rotation.
NetworkPrivate connectivity where justified, controlled egress and explicit provider allowlists.
IsolationSeparate tenants, environments, logs, caches and high-risk execution paths.
ThreatsAddress prompt injection, data exfiltration, tool abuse, model endpoint spoofing and denial of wallet.
ResponseProvide route disablement, credential revocation, evidence preservation and tested containment.

9. Operational Considerations

Availability

Deploy active-active across failure domains, remove local state, test dependency failure and publish service objectives.

Scaling

Scale on requests, tokens, concurrency, streaming connections, policy latency and provider quotas—not CPU alone.

Backup and DR

Back up policy, routing and audit configuration; rebuild ephemeral runtime; rehearse region and provider failover.

Support

Operate with service ownership, runbooks, escalation, on-call coverage, vendor contacts and business communications.

Versioning

Version APIs, policies, routes, model aliases and safety configurations independently with compatibility rules.

Maintenance

Patch dependencies, rotate secrets, remove deprecated models and test policy changes before promotion.

15. Common Anti-Patterns

Gateway as a pass-through proxy

Adds latency and cost without applying policy, evidence, routing or operational value.

One policy for every use case

Ignores differences in data sensitivity, autonomy, customer impact and regulatory exposure.

Provider keys in applications

Allows direct access that bypasses central controls and makes revocation difficult.

Logging every payload

Creates a sensitive-data store without clear purpose, access or retention controls.

Unvalidated fallback

Routes to a cheaper or available model whose quality and safety are not equivalent.

Gateway replaces governance

Technology cannot decide business ownership, acceptable risk or lawful purpose.

16. Decision Matrix

Use an enterprise AI Gateway when…Avoid or defer when…Consider alternatives when…
Several applications or teams use AI services.There is one isolated, low-risk experiment with no production commitment.Existing API management can enforce the complete required AI control set.
Models or providers must change without application rewrites.The organisation has no accountable platform owner or operating capacity.A managed platform provides equivalent controls with acceptable lock-in.
Policy, data, cost and audit controls must be consistent.The gateway would become an unresilient mandatory dependency.Workloads must remain fully isolated by regulation or criticality.
Agents require governed tool and model access.Required identity and classification context cannot be supplied.Local sidecars are necessary for disconnected or edge operation.

Should my organisation implement this?

Strong candidate

Medium-to-large portfolio, multiple providers, regulated or sensitive use cases, growing AI maturity and need for shared controls.

Adopt carefully

Moderate maturity, limited platform skills or uncertain demand. Start with a managed capability and a narrow service scope.

Defer

Single non-production experiment, low organisational readiness, no service owner or no ability to operate a critical shared dependency.
