Stage 01

AI Governance Strategy, Vision & Executive Mandate

Establish why governance exists, the authority it carries, and the enterprise outcomes it must protect.

Core activities

  • Define AI ambition, value thesis, governance vision, principles, and scope.
  • Set risk appetite by use-case impact, autonomy, data sensitivity, and regulatory exposure.
  • Confirm executive sponsor, funding authority, exception authority, and escalation route.
  • Translate business drivers into measurable governance objectives and success measures.

Exit criteria

  • Executive mandate and funding approved.
  • Scope includes internally built, embedded, purchased, and agentic AI.
  • Risk appetite is usable by intake and approval teams.
  • Governance principles are accepted as design constraints.

Stage 02

Current-State Assessment & Governance Baseline

Create an evidence-based view of maturity, AI usage, shadow AI, existing controls, and priority exposure.

Assessment domains

  • Strategy, governance, operating model, data, technology, security, privacy, legal, risk, compliance, and talent.
  • Existing and planned AI systems, embedded AI features, third-party services, models, agents, and automation.
  • Current approval pathways, policy coverage, incident history, assurance evidence, and control ownership.

Baseline decisions

  • Determine current and target maturity by domain.
  • Classify immediate risks requiring containment before the full model is operational.
  • Prioritize quick wins, foundational dependencies, and transformation workstreams.
  • Establish the initial enterprise AI system register.

Stage 04

Roles, RACI & Decision Rights

Remove ambiguity by defining who proposes, evaluates, approves, accepts risk, supplies evidence, and remains accountable.

Accountability design

  • Business owns value, outcomes, customer impact, and residual business risk.
  • Governance Office owns framework operation, orchestration, reporting, and evidence quality.
  • Architecture, Data, Cybersecurity, Privacy, Legal, Risk, Model Owners, and Operations own specialist decisions and controls.
  • Internal Audit independently evaluates design and operating effectiveness.

Decision-rights tests

  • Every lifecycle gate has one accountable decision owner.
  • Risk acceptance cannot be delegated to the delivery team creating the exposure.
  • Exceptions are time-bound, evidenced, and traceable to an authorized approver.
  • Conflicts between value, risk, architecture, and compliance have a named escalation route.

Stage 07

AI Risk Management Framework

Apply proportional governance based on impact, autonomy, recoverability, data, regulation, and affected stakeholders.

Risk taxonomy

  • Operational and behavioural risk.
  • Security, privacy, data, and confidentiality risk.
  • Regulatory, legal, intellectual-property, and third-party risk.
  • Model, fairness, explainability, reliability, and human-oversight risk.

Risk mechanics

  • Inherent risk, control effectiveness, residual risk, and acceptance authority.
  • Risk thresholds tied to confidence, human review, and automation authority.
  • Issues, exceptions, incidents, remediation, and formal closure evidence.
  • Portfolio aggregation to identify concentration and systemic dependencies.

Stage 08

Architecture, Data, Security & Technical Governance

Translate governance decisions into enforceable platform patterns, runtime controls, and technical evidence.

Architecture and platform controls

  • Deny-by-default model and tool routing through an enterprise AI control plane.
  • Identity, environment, scenario, prompt, document, and policy resolution.
  • Evidence gates, approved routing, response validation, human approval, immutable audit, and kill-switch capability.
  • Scalability, resilience, observability, cost, and technology lifecycle controls.

Data, security, and vendor controls

  • Ownership, classification, quality, lineage, minimization, masking, retention, and deletion.
  • Threat modelling, secure development, access control, vulnerability management, monitoring, and incident response.
  • Vendor due diligence, model isolation, training-use restrictions, regional processing, subcontractors, exit, and portability.

Stage 09

Implementation, Enablement & Adoption

Launch the operating model through controlled pilots, role-based enablement, workflow integration, and measurable adoption.

Mobilization

  • Establish workstreams, dependencies, milestones, governance forums, and delivery reporting.
  • Configure intake, reviews, approvals, evidence repositories, registers, and workflow automation.
  • Pilot low-, medium-, and high-risk use cases to validate proportional governance.
  • Resolve operating friction before enterprise rollout.

Enablement

  • Role-based learning for executives, business owners, control functions, architects, engineers, validators, and operations.
  • Practical guidance, office hours, reusable patterns, communications, and adoption measures.
  • Change impacts tracked across process, accountability, technology, data, and culture.

Stage 10

Monitoring, Assurance & Continuous Improvement

Demonstrate that governance is operating effectively and adapt it as the portfolio, technology, regulation, and risk environment change.

Management information

  • Portfolio growth, adoption, risk distribution, high-risk systems, exceptions, incidents, overdue actions, control adherence, model health, and value realization.
  • Leading indicators show emerging exposure; lagging indicators confirm realized outcomes.
  • Metrics have owners, thresholds, escalation rules, and decision use.

Three Lines assurance

  • First line performs controls and retains evidence.
  • Second line sets policy, challenges decisions, monitors risk, and tests selected controls.
  • Third line independently evaluates design and operating effectiveness.
  • Lessons from incidents, audit, regulation, and portfolio change feed framework improvement.
