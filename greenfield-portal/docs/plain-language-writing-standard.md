# Plain-language writing standard

## Purpose

Portal content must help a decision-maker understand the business issue before presenting implementation detail. Technical depth is retained, but it is introduced in layers.

## Required sequence

For major guidance, use this order where it fits:

1. Business meaning and one-sentence purpose.
2. Why the issue matters and who owns the decision.
3. Plain-language explanation.
4. Formal technical term and its full name.
5. Implementation detail and evidence.
6. Risks, controls, and exceptions.
7. Decision guidance, including when not to proceed.

## Terminology

- Expand an abbreviation at first use on every page: “Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).”
- Explain a specialised term in the sentence where it first affects a decision.
- Link the first meaningful use of a central term to the glossary when that helps the reader.
- Use the abbreviation alone after it has been introduced on that page.
- Preserve standards and regulatory names precisely.

## Style

- Prefer a direct subject and verb.
- Name the owner, action, evidence, and decision.
- Replace vague claims with measurable outcomes or explicit limits.
- Avoid promotional filler such as “seamless,” “cutting-edge,” and “unlock value.”
- Use short paragraphs, descriptive headings, lists for decisions, and tables for genuine comparisons.
- Distinguish requirements from recommendations and examples.

## Page review checklist

- The purpose and business outcome are visible before technical detail.
- Every important abbreviation is expanded at first use.
- Risks and controls are paired.
- Claims identify assumptions or evidence.
- The reader can tell when to use the approach, when not to use it, and who decides.
- Headings form a logical hierarchy.
- Links, glossary anchors, and accessible names work.
